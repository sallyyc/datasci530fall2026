Introduction¶

This report examines actions taken on proposed legislation during the 116th United States Congress. The dataset includes descriptions of events such as committee reviews, floor actions, and proposed amendments. Each row records one action associated with a bill, so the same bill may appear several times as it moves through the legislative process (different actions). The analysis begins with some basic descriptives of the data. It then examines House floor actions involving Senate bills and Senate amendments proposed on the floor. Using regular expressions(finding text patterns)the analysis extracts senator names and explores how frequently they appear and which senators are mentioned together.

Key Findings:¶

After removing 49 identical records, the dataset contains 3,254 action records associated with 1,494 distinct legislative measures. The selected Senate amendment actions involve 131 measures, most mentioning two distinct senators; McConnell has the most different second-mentioned senators under the first-two-names approach.The additional analysis finds slightly longer purpose descriptions for other amendments mentioning two senators, while substitute descriptions remain similarly short because they use a repeated phrase.

  • Looking at the DataViewer, I found 8 distinct bill types. About

67% of rows have the type "hr" (House bills), and about 20% have the type "s" (Senate bills). The other types represent different kinds of resolutions.

  • Different bill types can share the same number. For example, H.R. 1

and S. 1 are different bills, even though both have bill number 1. If I use only Congress and bill number, they are counted together.

  • Using Congress and bill number gives 1,331 unique identifiers.

Including bill type increases the count to 1,494 because it keeps these different measures separate.

  • I used Congress, bill type, and bill number to identify each distinct

measure. This count includes both bills and resolutions in the dataset, so repeated action records for the same measure are counted only once.

Extracting names and counting bills¶

What is the distribution of total bills proposed per person?¶

Process

I used regular expressions to extract names following Mr., Ms., or Mrs. The pattern captures the text between the title and either “moved” or “asked” words in the action description. This keeps names with multiple word names, such as Jackson Lee, together.

I checked whether any actions were missing an extracted name. I then created a dataset counting 'distinct' bills mentioning each person. I counted the number of distinct bills per person as one bill can have multiple action records mentioning the same person. So, repeated actions for the same bill and person count only once.

Bill number is sufficient to get the distinct bills in this subset since all records are Senate bills from the same Congress. These counts show bills mentioning a person in House floor actions.

New insight: Amendment purpose length vs. senators mentioned¶

For my additional analysis, I looked at whether amendment descriptions are longer when more senators are mentioned. I also compared substitute amendments specifically with other amendments to see whether the pattern changes between the two groups. (Substitute amendment: proposes replacement text for a bill or another amendment)

I focused on Senate amendments proposed on the floor so that this analysis builds on the senator names extracted in the previous analysis. Also, keeping one action type makes the descriptions more comparable, therefore, these findings apply to this subset, rather than all amendments.

Counting the entire action description can be misleading because the most descriptions include senator names and Congressional Record references (in parentheses). So, mentioning another senator would automatically add words and makes the analysis cloudy. To make the comparison more accurate, I only counted the text describing the amendment's purpose in the action column.

Process explanation¶

Most action descriptions place the amendment's purpose after a closing parenthesis containing Congressional Record references. I split the text at that parenthesis and kept the remaining description.

Two records do not have that parenthesis. For those records, I kept the text after the introductory sentence instead and checked for missing or empty descriptions after this step for all actions.

I classified amendments containing “In the nature of a substitute” as substitute amendments. I looked at descriptions containing the word “substitute” and found the phrase “In the nature of a substitute.” (used this specific phrase to identify substitute amendments as searching for “substitute” alone could include other uses of the word unrelated to topic) All remaining amendments were placed in the other amendment group. I counted words by splitting the purpose text at spaces.

Findings¶

I compared average purpose length within each amendment type. In this subset, there were only one or two senators mentioned for each action.

For other amendments, purpose descriptions average 13.20 words when one senator is mentioned and 14.56 words when two senators are mentioned. This suggests that descriptions of purpose of the amendment action with two senators tend to be somewhat longer within this group.

Substitute amendment descriptions average approximately 6 words for both senator counts. I believe this reflects the repeated phrase “In the nature of a substitute” used rather than a detailed explanation of the amendment unlike other admendment types.

The amendment type therefore matters when comparing description lengths. The other amendments show a some difference between senator counts, while substitute descriptions have the identitcal lengths.

These results do not establish that amendments mentioning two senators are more complex. The counts reflect the description supplied in the dataset. The differnce in description length for 1 and 2 numbers of senators mentioned might indicate someone proposing an amendment on behalf of another senator rather than equal involvement in writing it.
